## 2. What this means for you

Your opener said "the corporate VC model you helped create in the Gulf." That's not quite his story. He's a market-entry operator with a VC background. Don't repeat the line on the call. Reframe to his actual business.

**The real opportunity is a channel partnership, not one client.**

DBC's entire customer base is foreign companies landing in Qatar. Every one of them has the same problem the week after the licence is issued: no pipeline, no local sales team, and a founder or country manager who can't justify local hires yet. That is exactly your product.

He also has a second problem you solve: the companies he's set up need to show traction to stay in Qatar, and he's the sponsor on the licence. Dead companies are bad for him.

**Three ways this goes:**
1. **Referral partner.** DBC refers newly licensed clients to Vanguard for outbound. You pay a referral fee or revenue share.
2. **Bundled offer.** "Licence + first 90 days of pipeline." DBC sells it, Vanguard delivers. His margin on top of yours.
3. **Direct client.** DBC itself needs outbound to foreign companies considering Qatar. Lowest value, but the easiest first deal and it proves the product to him.

Push for 1 or 2. Offer 3 as the proof point if he's hesitant.

**3–15 min. Discovery.** Find the gap after the licence.
1. "What does a typical client look like? Sector, size, where they're coming from?"
2. "Once the licence is issued and the LLC is live, what happens in their first 90 days commercially?"
3. "How many of them arrive with a contract already in hand versus arriving to go find business?"
4. "The ones who struggle, what's the usual reason? Is it product, pricing, or they just don't have anyone making calls?"
5. "When a client asks you 'who can help us find customers here,' what do you tell them today?"
6. "How many new companies are you setting up per month right now?"

Question 5 is the one. Question 6 tells you the size of the channel.

**15–22 min. Position.** Only after he's described the gap.
> "That gap after the licence is exactly where we sit. A company lands in Qatar, has no pipeline and can't justify local headcount yet. We stand up a two- or three-person SDR team in a few weeks, Arabic and English, working Gulf hours, on a monthly fee. They keep the closers and the relationship. We feed them qualified meetings. For your clients it's the difference between a licence that produces revenue and one that sits there."

**22–28 min. The partnership ask.**
> "Here's what I'm thinking. Your clients need pipeline the day the licence is issued. We could be the team you point them to, either as a referral with a fee back to DBC, or as something you bundle into your own offer. Which of those would make sense on your side?"

Let him react. If he's interested in the bundle, that's the bigger business. If he wants referral only, take it and build from there.

**28–30 min. Close.**
